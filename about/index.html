The story behind the book

My Romans Road

By Landon Key

From the author

Friendship, a journey to Honduras, and a notebook of names.

Explore the book →

The story behind this book began with a prayer that God would use my workplace as a mission opportunity. Shortly afterward, Jared joined our team.

Over the next three and a half years, we became close friends. I prayed for him and regularly shared my faith with him, often through ordinary conversations over lunch. After graduating from college, Jared accepted a job at another company. About a year later, he met Katie, who would become his wife.

Katie loved Orphanage Emmanuel in Honduras. As their relationship grew serious, she wanted Jared to come to love that place too. After his first trip, I could see that God had gripped his heart in a life-changing way.

Years later, Adrienne and I lost our second child, our daughter Allie, because of complications during pregnancy.

Before we lost Allie, God’s providence had brought me to Romans 8:18 in my personal Bible study. I had been seeking and praying to understand that passage, little knowing that God, in his kindness and wisdom, was preparing my heart for a loss I did not see coming. Romans 8:18 became an anchor for my soul. Though I was in deep despair, through that heartache I would learn to hope in the weight of his glory.

In the wake of losing Allie, we felt drawn by God to visit the children at Emmanuel. Jared invited us to join him and Katie on their next trip. Adrienne and I talked it over and decided to go.

In Honduras, God made clear to me that he was calling me to preach. Romans was heavy on my heart. It was a book I had fallen in love with again and again.

The children at Emmanuel would ask me to tell them their names. They wanted to be known and remembered. I had always struggled to remember names, so I carried a notebook and a pen and let the children write their names down. Every night before bed, I went over those names again and again so I would remember them when I saw the children the next day.

Through those interactions, God showed me that names mattered far more than I had understood.

Later, as I prepared to preach through Romans at my home church, I read chapter 16 with those children and the names in my notebook on my mind. I had read Romans many times before, but this time something clicked: the names mattered because the people mattered.

The gospel is preached to people God knows by name, people we ought to seek to know by name too.

That is how this study developed: through ordinary lunch conversations with a friend, through seeing God change his life, and through his invitation to join him in Honduras. I found myself in an unfamiliar culture, among people whose language I did not yet know. I would later learn their language to know them more deeply. There, God called me to preach and teach, and the children's desire to be remembered helped me see something in Romans I had missed.

This book continues to remind me that God is calling a people to himself, and he calls them by name.
