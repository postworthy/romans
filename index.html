The book

Theology for the life you actually live.

Romans asks questions that reach into the way we live. What grounds our confidence before God? How does grace change our relationship to sin? How do we love someone whose conscience differs from our own?

Developed from an expository sermon series, this book follows the movement of Paul’s letter across eighteen chapters. It brings theological explanation and pastoral application together, with six appendices for questions that invite a closer look.

  1. Follow the whole letter

    Read the argument as it develops across Romans, keeping its great themes connected.

  2. Give difficult questions careful attention

    Explore faith, freedom, assurance, Israel, obedience, and mission in the course of the study.

  3. Keep people in view

    Consider how the teaching takes shape in relationships, work, suffering, worship, and service.

The story behind the book

It began with names in a notebook.

At Orphanage Emmanuel in Honduras, the children wanted me to remember their names. I carried a notebook so they could write them down, and each night I studied those names before seeing the children again the next day.

Later, as I prepared to preach through Romans, I read chapter 16 with those children in mind. The names mattered because the people mattered. That realization helped shape the sermon series and the book that grew from it.

— Landon Key

A few questions before you begin.

What kind of book is this?

It is a guided study through Romans, developed from an expository sermon series. Its emphasis is theological explanation and pastoral application, rather than the format of a technical commentary.

Does it cover all of Romans?

Yes. Eighteen chapters follow the letter from its opening announcement of the gospel to its closing greetings and doxology. Six appendices explore related subjects in more depth.

Which Bible translation does it use?

Scripture quotations in the book are from the English Standard Version (ESV).

Can I read it with others?

You can use the chapters as shared reading and discuss them alongside the biblical text. A separate discussion guide is not currently available.
