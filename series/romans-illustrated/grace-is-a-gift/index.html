Why does Paul call grace a gift?

Think about a paycheck. You worked for it, so your employer owes you those wages.

In Romans four, Paul uses that contrast to explain righteousness before God. A wage is earned. A gift is received.

God justifies the ungodly who trust him. Faith receives what God gives through Christ; it does not put God in our debt.

That gift came through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. Grace is free to us because Christ paid the cost.

As you read Romans, notice where Paul excludes boasting. Then thank God for the righteousness you could never earn.

A gift leaves no room for boasting

Romans 3:21–26 locates justification in the redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ. Romans 4 then contrasts a worker’s pay with righteousness counted to the one who trusts God. Paul uses Abraham and David to show that this grace belongs to the story Scripture has been telling all along.

Landon’s chapter on justification develops faith as receiving God’s promise. Gratitude and obedience grow from the mercy we have received. They cannot turn that mercy into a debt God owes us.

Pause & consider

Where are you tempted to measure your welcome with God by what you have earned?
