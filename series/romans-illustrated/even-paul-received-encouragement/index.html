Who could encourage an apostle?

In Romans one, Paul longs to visit the believers in Rome and strengthen them.

Then he explains that they will be mutually encouraged by each other’s faith, both theirs and his.

Paul expects to receive encouragement too. The faith of ordinary believers matters even to an apostle.

This helps us see service as something we share. God brings people together who can strengthen one another.

So when you speak with someone at church this week, ask how they are trusting Christ. Listen for what their faith can teach you.

Share honestly too. Faithfulness includes giving encouragement and receiving it. Your place in that conversation matters.

Encouragement can travel both ways

In Romans 1:11–12, Paul explains his desire to strengthen the church by speaking of encouragement through each other’s faith. His apostolic calling leaves room for receiving encouragement from the people he serves.

The book’s opening discussion of Paul’s pastoral posture makes this a practical picture of shared life. A person can have real responsibility and still need the faith, prayers and presence of others. Acknowledging that need gives other members of Christ’s body room to serve.

Pause & consider

Who encourages you, and do they know what their faith has meant to you?
