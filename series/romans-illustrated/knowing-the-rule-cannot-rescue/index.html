Why do I know better, yet still sin?

Romans seven shows us something painful: knowing the command does not give us the power to obey it.

God’s law is holy and good. It exposes sin, including the desires we would rather leave unnamed. But a diagnosis cannot heal the disease.

Paul’s cry is, who will deliver me? His answer points us to Jesus Christ.

Romans eight says God has done what the law, weakened by the flesh, could not do. He sent his Son.

When Scripture exposes your sin, be honest about it. Bring it to Christ. Your hope for rescue rests in him, even when you feel the depth of your need.

A good command cannot supply new life

Romans 7 insists that the law is holy and good while describing how sin uses the commandment. Knowledge exposes the problem but cannot itself deliver the person caught in it. The movement into Romans 8 brings God’s action in Christ and the Spirit into view.

The book follows that argument from the cry for deliverance to life by the Spirit. Christians differ over aspects of the speaker’s identity in Romans 7, but the passage’s dependence on Christ should stay central. We need more than another reminder of a rule.
