If Christ has freed me from sin, why am I still tempted?

Romans six distinguishes sin’s presence from its mastery. Paul tells believers not to let sin reign, because it still seeks their obedience.

But those united to Christ share in his death and resurrection. Their old self was crucified with him. They belong to a new life.

That is why Paul says to consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus.

This means learning to think according to what God has done. Temptation does not have the right to command you.

When it comes, remember whose you are. Offer your words and actions to God as someone he has brought from death to life.

Freedom changes whom we serve

Romans 6 connects new life with union with Christ in his death and resurrection. Paul still commands believers to refuse sin’s reign and present themselves to God. Those commands make sense within a real transfer of allegiance.

The book explains how temptation can remain a struggle while sin has lost its rightful claim to rule. Words, habits and bodily actions can now be offered to God in dependence on grace. Freedom calls us into active obedience.
