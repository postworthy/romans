Why did Paul want to preach the gospel to people who already believed?

In Romans one, he thanks God because their faith is known throughout the world. Yet he is eager to preach the gospel to them.

They already belong to Christ, and they still need to hear about him: who he is, how he died and rose, and what God has promised.

The same good news that awakens faith continues to nourish it.

So when a familiar passage comes up, slow down. Ask what it teaches you about Christ.

Christian maturity includes hearing the gospel again, understanding it more deeply, and learning to live from it.

The gospel keeps strengthening believers

Paul calls the Roman Christians loved by God and called to belong to Christ. He thanks God for their faith, then says he is eager to preach the gospel to them. His eagerness makes sense when we see the gospel as the power of God for salvation and the continuing ground of faith.

The opening chapters of Landon’s book trace that connection. Believers keep needing to hear who Jesus is and what God has done through him. Returning to the gospel can deepen assurance, undo boasting and equip us to love the people around us.
