Why would I help someone who hurt me?

Romans 12:20–21; 5:8–10

Read the narration

Why would you help someone who hasn’t even apologized?

Romans twelve says that if your enemy is hungry, feed him. If he’s thirsty, give him something to drink.

Paul calls us to meet a real need with good. Kindness isn’t a way to buy an apology or make the other person change.

Romans reminds us that God reconciled us to himself through Christ while we were enemies. We’ve received mercy we didn’t earn.

That mercy can shape a wise, appropriate act of good towards someone who has wronged you. You can care without pretending trust is restored.

The outcome belongs to God. Your part is to refuse revenge and let received mercy become mercy you extend.

Goodness that is not a bargain

Romans 12:20–21 gives the concrete example of feeding a hungry enemy and giving drink to a thirsty one. This follows Paul’s command to leave vengeance to God. A real need becomes an opportunity to do good.

The book connects such love with the mercy God has shown sinners. Kindness can be offered without making another person’s gratitude the price of it. Wise boundaries and accountability can remain necessary while we refuse to let revenge govern our actions.

Pause & consider

What good could you do without making the other person’s response the measure of faithfulness?
