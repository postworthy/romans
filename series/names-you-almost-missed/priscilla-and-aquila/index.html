They made room for the church.

Priscilla & Aquila • Romans 16:3–5

Illustrative reconstruction

A church in their house.

“Greet also the church in their house.”

Romans 16:5 (ESV)

Before this greeting, a disrupted life.

Acts says the couple came from Italy after Claudius ordered Jews to leave Rome. In Corinth, Paul stayed and worked with them as a fellow tentmaker.

Acts 18:1–3 • paraphrase

Work. Teaching. Welcome.

They explained God’s way more accurately to Apollos. First Corinthians also sends greetings from a church in their house, in Paul’s Ephesian setting.

Acts 18:26; 1 Corinthians 16:8,19

The house is real. Its address is unknown.

The letters name a household gathering. They don’t give us a floor plan, a congregation size, or a securely identified building we can visit.

1 Corinthians 16:19; Romans 16:5

Gratitude reached beyond one city.

Paul calls them coworkers in Christ Jesus. They risked their lives for him; Paul and the Gentile churches give thanks. The rescue itself isn’t described.

Romans 16:3–4 • paraphrase

Who made room for your faith?

A patient conversation. A welcome at the table. A place in a church family. Remember someone whose ordinary availability helped you belong.

A modern invitation, not their recorded biography

Honor someone who welcomed you.

If you’d like, name a person or a couple and one way they made room for you. Tell them what that welcome meant.

You can begin with a private thank-you.

Scripture & the people behind the names

A partnership across several places

Acts 18 follows Priscilla and Aquila from their arrival from Italy into shared work with Paul at Corinth and service at Ephesus. Romans 16:3–5 later thanks them for risking their lives and greets the church in their house. The letter gives no account of the particular rescue.

Priscilla and Prisca are forms of the same woman’s name. The couple’s teaching of Apollos and the house-church notices show several forms of partnership. Their differing name order across passages does not by itself establish rank, wealth or which spouse had greater teaching ability.

Pause & consider

Which shared place in your life could become a place of faithful partnership?
