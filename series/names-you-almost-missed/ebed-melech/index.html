He spoke up, then brought the cloth.

Jeremiah 38:7–13; 39:15–18

Care reached the smallest detail.

“Put the rags and clothes between your armpits and the ropes.”

Jeremiah 38:12 (ESV) · excerpt

He went to the king.

When Ebed-melech heard what had happened to Jeremiah, he told Zedekiah that the men had acted wickedly. Jeremiah had been left in a muddy cistern and was in danger of dying there.

Jeremiah 38:6–9

He thought about the ropes.

The king authorized a rescue. Ebed-melech gathered men and old clothes, then lowered the cloth to Jeremiah. He told him where to place it before they drew him up with the ropes.

Jeremiah 38:10–13

An Ethiopian in the royal household.

Jeremiah calls Ebed-melech an Ethiopian and identifies him as a eunuch within the royal household. These details place him in Judah’s story. The passage gives no portrait or account of his earlier life.

Jeremiah 38:7 · setting and limits

The Lord sent a word for him.

During Jeremiah’s confinement, God gave him a message for Ebed-melech: he would be delivered in the coming disaster because he had trusted the Lord. It was a promise to this man in that crisis.

Jeremiah 39:15–18

Speak with courage. Care with attention.

When someone is being mistreated, seek a safe, truthful way to help. Attend to practical needs as well as the larger wrong, and work with others who can help.

A present-day invitation

Who noticed the detail that mattered?

Think of someone who both spoke for you and made the help gentler. Thank them for the particular care you remember. Public naming is optional.
