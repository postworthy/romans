Romans 16:13 (ESV) • excerpt

We do not have her name.

Paul greets Rufus’s mother, then speaks of her relationship to himself: she has been a mother to him as well.

Romans 16:13; see source notes

Paul remembered the relationship.

Her name is absent from our reading, yet Paul remembers her as a mother to him. Her care mattered within a relationship we cannot fully reconstruct.

Romans 16:13; see source notes

A relationship is not a biological claim.

The verse identifies her as Rufus’s mother. Paul’s additional description does not establish that she was his own birth mother.

Historical context and limits • source notes

The weight of personal care.

Love takes another person’s good seriously. Her greeting invites us to consider the lasting weight of personal care, even when the details never reach a wider audience.

Romans 12:10; 16:13; Philippians 2:3–4 • comparison

Be present to someone nearby.

Who we are to the people nearest us deserves our care. Honor someone whose steady affection has helped you, then consider how you can be present to another person.

A present-day invitation

Whose name comes to mind?

Whose care stays with you, though most people have never heard their name? Share a first name and a true memory, if you’d like. A private thank-you matters, too.

Her care receives its own greeting

Romans 16:13 greets Rufus’s mother and recognizes that she had been a mother to Paul as well. She is remembered through a relationship, yet the care she gave receives personal acknowledgment.

Paul does not name a particular meal, home or occasion, and he gives no reason for omitting her personal name. Her identification through Rufus would have made sense within the receiving community. Being unnamed to later readers does not mean she was unknown among the people whose lives she shared.
