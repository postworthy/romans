Romans 16:14 (ESV) • excerpt

Read all five names.

Asyncritus. Phlegon. Hermes. Patrobas. Hermas. Paul asks the church to greet each, and the brothers who are with them.

Romans 16:14; see source notes

Others with them. Real people.

The greeting includes brothers whose names we never read. The words point toward a community able to recognize the people Paul means, beyond the five names on the page.

Romans 16:14; see source notes

A group, not a recovered address.

This cluster may reflect a gathering of believers. The verse does not explicitly name a house church, locate a building or tell us how these people are related.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Belonging asks something of us.

Romans calls us away from pride and toward belonging to one another. A small circle of people who know us carries real responsibilities of affection, presence and honor.

Romans 12:3–5,10,16; 16:14 • comparison

Care for your actual community.

If the wish to be remembered widely is making you less available nearby, pause. Reach out to someone in the community that has helped you grow.

A present-day invitation

Whose name comes to mind?

Who in your small circle could use your time this week? Share a first name and a true memory, if you’d like. A private thank-you matters, too.

Five names and a wider company

Romans 16:14 names Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermes, Patrobas and Hermas, then greets the brothers with them. The wider company belongs to the greeting even though the letter does not list every name.

The wording may suggest a recognizable gathering, but it supplies no address and appoints no first-named person as its pastor. Nor can Hermas be securely identified with the author associated with The Shepherd of Hermas. We encounter a community with more people and relationships than the surviving sentence can describe.
