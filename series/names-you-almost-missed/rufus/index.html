Chosen in the Lord.

Paul greets Rufus with this description. Romans does not give him an office or tell us that he matters more than the other believers being greeted.

His mother is remembered, too.

Paul also greets Rufus’s mother and says she has been a mother to him. Her care is a distinct part of this greeting.

Is he the Rufus in Mark?

Mark 15:21 names a Rufus as Simon of Cyrene’s son. That is a possible connection; neither passage explicitly identifies the two men as the same person.

A name, a mother and a possible connection

Romans 16:13 greets Rufus as chosen in the Lord, then honors his mother’s care for Paul. The greeting locates him within relationships of faith and care.

Mark 15:21 also names a Rufus, a son of Simon of Cyrene. Identifying the two is possible, but the shared name does not prove it. We should therefore keep that connection conditional rather than use Simon’s encounter with the cross as an established chapter in this Rufus’s biography.
