Romans 16:23 (ESV) • excerpt

The relationship comes first.

Quartus joins the greetings and is called our brother. The verse gives him a place in Christian fellowship without a title or list of achievements.

Romans 16:23; see source notes

No task is supplied.

We do not know what Quartus did in this setting. The absence of a named assignment does not make him an interchangeable extra.

Romans 16:23; see source notes

Fourth does not identify a life story.

The Latin ordinal name does not prove enslavement, birth order or that Tertius was his brother. Their shared context is not a family record.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

Romans describes shared life in God’s family and one body. Quartus’s brother-language gives this belonging a personal name.

Romans 8:14–17; 12:4–5; 16:23 • comparison

Remember someone whose care mattered.

Tell someone that you are glad to share life in Christ with them. Gratitude can honor belonging as well as a particular accomplishment.

A present-day invitation

Whose name comes to mind?

Who would be encouraged to hear, “I’m grateful you’re part of our life together”? Share a first name and a true memory, if you’d like. A private thank-you matters, too.

Brother is enough for a greeting

Romans 16:23 calls Quartus our brother. Paul records no office, achievement or personal story for him. The relationship within the family of faith is itself sufficient to give him a place in the letter.

His ordinal name cannot establish birth order, legal status or a family connection to Tertius. Rather than fill the silence with an invented biography, we can let the greeting challenge our tendency to rank people by visibility. A person may be deeply known within a smaller circle than the historical record preserves.
