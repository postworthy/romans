Acts 9:36–43

A disciple known for care.

“She was full of good works and acts of charity.”

Acts 9:36 (ESV) · excerpt

Two names, one disciple.

Acts calls her Tabitha and explains that the name translates as Dorcas. She lived in Joppa. Her story begins with the good she was doing among people who knew her.

Acts 9:36

They could show what she had made.

When she died, the widows wept and showed Peter tunics and other garments she had made while she was with them. Their grief had particular memories.

Acts 9:37–39

Care they could hold in their hands.

The garments connect her skill with her neighbors. The widows could point to things she had made. Luke gives us this glimpse of useful work in the life of believers in Joppa.

Text and its limits · Acts 9:36–39

Her story continues with the Lord’s mercy.

Peter knelt and prayed. Tabitha opened her eyes, and he presented her alive to the believers and widows. The news spread, and many believed in the Lord.

Acts 9:40–42

Notice a useful skill given in love.

Think of something you can make, repair or share. Ask someone what would actually help. Let their need guide the kindness you offer.

A present-day invitation

Whose care can you still remember?

A mended coat? A meal when you were tired? Recall one true act and thank the person who offered it. Public naming is optional; private gratitude matters too.

The garments make her care visible

Acts 9:36–43 identifies Tabitha, also called Dorcas, as a disciple in Joppa known for good works and charity. When she dies, the widows show Peter garments she made. Their grief and the clothing together give a concrete picture of her service.

Peter prays and she is restored to life; many in Joppa then believe in the Lord. The passage does not promise recovery to every generous disciple. It also leaves her age, marital status and any formal sewing business unspecified. Her care is visible enough without those additions.

Pause & consider

Whose practical care has left a visible mark on your life?
