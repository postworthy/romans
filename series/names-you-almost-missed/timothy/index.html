Other letters show his care.

Philippians 2 describes Timothy’s genuine concern and his proven service with Paul. First Thessalonians remembers him strengthening and encouraging believers in their faith.

A substantial record, not a complete diary.

Acts and the letters supply much more than a name. They do not give us his exact age at each stage or settle every historical question about the letters addressed to him.

A coworker among the senders

Romans 16:21 brings Timothy into the letter’s closing greetings as Paul’s fellow worker. The direction of the greeting matters: he is among those sending their love to Rome, rather than one of the people being addressed there.

This short notice gives an established partnership a place within the church’s shared correspondence. It does not by itself tell us Timothy’s exact task during the writing of Romans. We can recognize his service here without importing every possible detail from other stages of his ministry.
