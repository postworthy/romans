“Erastus, the city treasurer”

Romans 16:23 (ESV) • excerpt

Paul names a civic worker.

The ESV describes Erastus as the city treasurer. He joins the greeting from the sending network rather than appearing as another Roman recipient.

Romans 16:23; see source notes

The precise office is debated.

The Greek civic title has prompted different administrative identifications. Our English translation is not a complete Roman job description.

Romans 16:23; see source notes

A pavement names an Erastus.

A Corinth inscription records a benefaction connected with an aedileship. Whether that Erastus is Paul’s Erastus depends on debated dating, office and identification questions.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

The greeting can prompt us to notice service connected with civic work. It does not prove how Erastus carried out his duties or settle a modern political program.

Romans 12:4–8; 16:23 • comparison

A civic title and an unsettled inscription

Romans 16:23 identifies Erastus with a civic financial role. An inscription from Corinth also names an Erastus who paid for paving in connection with a public office. The overlap has attracted attention because it might connect a named believer to a surviving artifact.

The office, dating and personal identification remain debated. The inscription cannot simply be presented as proof of the biblical man’s career. Romans itself securely preserves a greeting from someone with a civic responsibility, reminding us that service to Christ reached into varied settings of ordinary work.
