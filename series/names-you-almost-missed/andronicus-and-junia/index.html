Romans 16:7 (ESV) • excerpt

Paul came after them.

Paul honors Andronicus and Junia as people who were in Christ before he was. His own calling does not erase those who believed before him.

Romans 16:7; see source notes

Their service had a cost.

He calls them his kinsmen and fellow prisoners. We should honor those words without inventing their arrest, a shared cell or the circumstances of imprisonment.

Romans 16:7; see source notes

Read the debated phrase carefully.

The ESV says they are well known to the apostles. Other translations read outstanding among the apostles. That disagreement deserves study alongside the shared commendations.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

Sober self-assessment and mutual honor fit a greeting in which an apostle openly acknowledges earlier believers. This connection is pastoral synthesis.

Scripture & the people behind the names

Faith before Paul’s conversion

Romans 16:7 remembers Andronicus and Junia as in Christ before Paul and as people who had shared imprisonment. The order of that history matters: the apostle can honor believers whose faith preceded his own.

Junia is widely understood as a woman’s name. The Greek expression concerning the apostles has been interpreted both as distinction among the apostles and as being well known to them. Neither their precise office nor the place of imprisonment should be treated as settled by a reconstructed scene. The verse does not explicitly call them husband and wife.
