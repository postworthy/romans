Read the greeting.

“Apelles, who is approved in Christ”

Romans 16:10 (ESV) • excerpt

Approved in Christ.

Paul greets Apelles with a distinctive commendation: approved in Christ. The words carry honor even though the verse does not describe its background.

Romans 16:10; see source notes

We cannot stage the ordeal.

The greeting does not tell us what Apelles endured. A prison, court, illness or act of persecution would be an invented scene.

Romans 16:10; see source notes

A name is not a profession.

Other ancient people shared this name. Nothing here identifies Paul’s Apelles with a famous painter or gives him that occupation.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

Romans speaks of hope and character amid suffering. That broader passage can inform application; it does not tell us which experience lies behind Apelles’s commendation.

Romans 5:1–5; 16:10 • comparison

Remember someone whose care mattered.

Think of a believer whose steady trust has encouraged you. Honor what you actually know, leaving private trials private.

A present-day invitation

Whose name comes to mind?

Whose quiet steadiness has helped you trust Christ? Share a first name and a true memory, if you’d like. A private thank-you matters, too.

Recognized faithfulness, an unknown trial

Romans 16:10 describes Apelles as approved in Christ. Paul recognizes something tested and valued in this believer, though he supplies no story of the testing.

We do not know whether the background involved persecution, a private hardship or some other circumstance. Inventing a prison scene would make the greeting more specific than it is. The distinction leaves room to honor faithfulness whose cost was understood by the people involved even when later readers cannot recover it.
