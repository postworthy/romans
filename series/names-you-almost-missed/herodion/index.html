Romans 16:11 (ESV) • excerpt

One name. One relationship.

Paul asks that Herodion be greeted and calls him his kinsman. The greeting gives us a connection to Paul without giving us a family tree.

Romans 16:11; see source notes

Kinship needs careful reading.

Paul uses kinship language for fellow Jews elsewhere. Here the exact degree of relationship is not stated, so we should not identify a cousin or other particular relative.

Romans 16:11; see source notes

Nearby names do not establish membership.

Herodion appears between household greetings. That placement does not prove he belonged to either establishment or to a royal family.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

Romans honors Israel’s story and also speaks of belonging to one body in Christ. This larger setting helps us consider kinship without inventing Herodion’s ancestry.

Kinship without a recovered family tree

Romans 16:11 calls Herodion Paul’s kinsman. In this chapter, the term can be understood in relation to shared Jewish identity; it does not provide a precise family relationship.

Herodion’s name alone cannot establish membership in the Herodian dynasty. Paul gives no conversion account or position of influence. The greeting allows us to value a person’s place among God’s people without making historical prominence the condition of that value.
