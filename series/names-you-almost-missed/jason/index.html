Romans 16:21 (ESV) • excerpt

Romans names him among the senders.

Jason sends a greeting with Paul’s companions and is included in the kinship description. That is the secure starting point for this Jason.

Romans 16:21; see source notes

Acts remembers a Jason who welcomed them.

In Acts 17, a Jason in Thessalonica is accused of receiving Paul and Silas. He faces public trouble and provides security before being released.

Romans 16:21; see source notes

The identification remains a proposal.

The Jason in Acts may be this man, but neither text explicitly joins the records. We can study the hospitality episode without silently making it his biography.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

Acts’ Jason episode can illuminate the cost of welcome as a comparison. The Romans greeting itself does not narrate those events.

Romans 12:13; Acts 17:5–9 • comparison

Thank someone who welcomed you when doing so was inconvenient or costly. Honor only the acts you actually know.

A possible connection held carefully

Romans 16:21 names Jason among those sending greetings with Paul. Acts 17 describes a Jason in Thessalonica whose house was caught up in opposition to the Christian message.

They may be the same man, but the shared name is not enough to make that identification certain. The Thessalonian story can be read as a possible connection, with its uncertainty stated. Jason’s secure place in Romans is already worth noticing: his greeting joins the relationships spanning Paul and the church in Rome.
