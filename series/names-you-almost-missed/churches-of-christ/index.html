Romans 16:16 (ESV) • excerpt

A greeting from beyond Rome.

Paul conveys greetings from the churches of Christ. His list of particular people opens toward a wider fellowship.

Romans 16:16; see source notes

The greeting asks for action.

The Roman believers are to greet one another. The holy-kiss language describes the embodied greeting in the text; the call should not be used to pressure physical contact today.

Romans 16:16; see source notes

Not a modern denominational label.

The phrase identifies churches belonging to Christ. It should not be turned into a claim that Paul is naming one present-day institution.

Historical context and limits • source notes

Read it within the larger letter.

The letter connects harmony in worship, welcome across peoples and the gospel made known to the nations. Local belonging has a larger horizon.

A local church receives a wider welcome

Romans 16:16 widens the greetings beyond the people already named: the churches of Christ greet the Roman believers. The holy kiss belongs to that expression of fellowship.

The greeting is not a census of congregations or the name of a modern denomination. Its welcome reaches across separate gatherings. Today the passage can encourage a warm and holy reception of others while respecting people’s boundaries and the differing ways communities express a greeting.
