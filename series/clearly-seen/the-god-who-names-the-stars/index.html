Read the narration

Do you ever feel too small to be noticed? Isaiah forty invites us to look at the stars. Their Creator calls them all by name.

Then Isaiah speaks to people who feel that God has overlooked them. The Maker of the whole earth never grows weary, and he gives strength to the weary. You can bring him the tiredness you've been carrying.

Waiting for the Lord may still mean taking one slow step through a difficult day. When you look up tonight, remember who made those stars, and ask him for strength to take your next step.

The stars and a weary people

Isaiah 40:26–31 addresses Jacob and Israel’s fear that their way is hidden from God. The prophet directs them to the Creator who calls the stars by name, then speaks of the strength he gives to the weary. The scale of the heavens serves a deeply personal reassurance.

The Christian application should preserve that original setting rather than erase it. Waiting on the Lord is not a technique for instant physical recovery. The passage gives tired people reason to depend on the God whose power and understanding exceed their own. The sky directs attention to him; Isaiah’s words explain the promise.

Pause & consider

Where do you need to hear that weariness has not made you invisible to God?
