Read the narration

A missionary's letters preserve the name of a woman whose help had reached him more than once. Her name was Eadburga.

Boniface thanked this abbess for books and garments that had comforted him in distress. The gratitude was personal. He remembered what her kindness had meant to him.

He also asked for a copy of Peter's letters written in gold. He wanted the people hearing him preach to see the honor given to Scripture. The request survives; it doesn't tell us that the book was completed.

Eadburga's repeated help gave Boniface reason to thank God. He could ask for what he needed because he had already known her kindness.

Mission depended on people who received support as well as those who supplied it. Books and practical provision could strengthen someone serving far away.

Is there a worker you could ask about an actual need? A thoughtful question may reveal something a general offer misses.

And if someone has helped you keep going, tell them what their kindness meant. A letter of thanks can honor work that others never see.

The books that supported a journey

Boniface’s letters thank Eadburga for books and church garments that had encouraged him in his work. Another letter asks for the letters of Peter to be copied in gold. The request reveals how a preacher serving far from home depended on other people’s resources and skill.

Edward Kylie’s 1911 translation prints these exchanges as letters XIV and XV; the corresponding Tangl numbers are 35 and 30. The gold manuscript is a requested commission, not a documented completed gift. The letters also do not establish that Eadburga personally copied every page.

Her precise identification with later accounts of particular abbesses remains debated. The attested exchange is enough to preserve a woman’s name within the practical support of mission. Romans 15 likewise allows us to see prayer and material help as participation in the work.
