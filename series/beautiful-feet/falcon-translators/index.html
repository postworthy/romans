Read the narration

Elida García de Falcón wrote the Spanish translations by hand. Her daughter Clotilde typed the pages.

Their shared work helped Spanish-speaking Methodist women use material prepared for the church's service. Elida brought gifts developed through teaching, poetry and newspaper writing in Texas. Clotilde helped those words become pages other women could use.

When illness interrupted her mother's work, Clotilde continued. Together, their contribution extended across twenty-six years.

Elida also translated hymns. Choosing words carefully could help a neighbor take part in worship and understand a task the church was doing together.

Her pastor remembered a home where people came for counsel, and where sick or needy neighbors found shelter. The woman working over a page also made room for people at her door.

Romans describes Christ's people as one body with different gifts. Each belongs through grace, and each has something to offer. A finished translation carried the labor of a mother and daughter, including work readers might never notice.

Who helps your gifts reach someone else? Think of the person who prepares a room, checks your words or carries on when your strength gives out. Thank them for what they actually do. You may have a part in helping their gifts reach others, too.

The handwriting and the typing both mattered

Elida García de Falcón translated program materials into Spanish by hand; her daughter Clotilde Falcón Náñez typed them. When illness interrupted Elida’s work, Clotilde continued. Their shared work helped Spanish-speaking Methodist women take part in the church’s service.

The biography records Elida’s program-book work from 1943 to 1964 and describes the combined translation effort across twenty-six years. Those are different spans, not a basis for inventing an exact final date. Her teaching, poetry, journalism and hymn translation form part of the wider background.

Remembering both women helps us see how a finished page can carry several people’s gifts. Translation, preparation and continuation each made the material usable. Romans 12 gives such shared service a place within the life of one body.
