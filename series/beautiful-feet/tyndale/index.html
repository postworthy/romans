William Tyndale

A lamp, a coat and a Hebrew Bible

Read the narration

William Tyndale was cold, and his clothes were wearing out. From prison, he asked for a warmer cap, a coat and cloth to mend his leggings. He also asked permission to have a lamp. Long evenings alone in darkness were wearing him down. Above everything else, he wanted a Hebrew Bible, a grammar and a dictionary, so he could keep studying.

Tyndale had helped bring a printed English New Testament into people's hands. Translating depended on friends, printers and people willing to carry the books. Now the man whose work helped others read Scripture needed help himself. His letter ends by entrusting himself to God and the grace of Jesus Christ.

Friends tried to help after his arrest. He was executed the following year. We remember his labor as a servant who trusted in Christ.

Romans says faith comes through hearing the message about Christ. People still need time and practical support to help others hear it.

Is there someone whose service you could strengthen? Ask what would help. A warm meal or a useful book can become part of another person's work for the gospel.

A request from prison

William Tyndale’s prison letter asks for warmer clothing, a lamp and Hebrew study materials. Its practical details bring a translator’s needs into view: bodily cold, the darkness of the evening and the desire to continue studying Scripture.

The surviving text is in Latin. The English version linked below is J. I. Mombert’s nineteenth-century translation, presented with the Latin. Tyndale requests a Hebrew Bible, grammar and dictionary; the letter does not establish that the materials reached him. His work on a printed English New Testament belongs within the longer history of English Bible translation, rather than being the first translation into English of any biblical text.

Romans 15:30–33 lets us consider the help and prayer a traveling worker needs. That is a present-day connection to Tyndale’s circumstances, rather than a claim about which passage prompted his request.
