Rota Waitoa

The pastor who went back to learn

Read the narration

Rota Waitoa sometimes returned to college after he'd become a pastor. He went back to read, study and spend time with a trusted friend. The responsibilities waiting for him were large, and he needed help with the questions they raised.

Years before his ordination, he'd managed provisions at Saint John's College while studying and teaching younger Maori pupils. In eighteen fifty-three, he became the first Maori ordained in the Anglican Church, after years of training and disagreement over Maori access to ministry.

On the East Coast, a local leader initially opposed his appointment. Later, Waitoa baptized him, and they worked together to build a church.

His later years included the East Coast wars. He opposed the King movement and Pai Marire, and became involved in hostilities.

Romans calls us to sober judgment about ourselves. We belong to one another in Christ, with different gifts received by grace.

Responsibility can make it hard to admit what we need to learn. A return to study, or an honest conversation with someone we trust, can strengthen our service.

What question in your responsibilities needs patient attention? Receive help, and let it become part of the care you offer others.

A ministry that still needed learning

Rota Waitoa became the first Māori ordained into the Anglican church in 1853 after years of study and service at St John’s College. His East Coast ministry brought responsibilities for which he continued to seek study, friendship and support.

G. J. Dempsey’s national biography describes his returns to St John’s to read and renew relationships. It also places his ministry within unequal colonial church structures and records his later involvement in wartime hostilities. Honoring his perseverance does not settle the moral questions surrounding those conflicts or make every missionary assessment of Māori leadership reliable.

The return to learning offers a bounded but useful picture: people carrying responsibility still need help. Romans 12 describes a body with differing gifts, and Romans 15 asks the strong to build up their neighbors. Receiving support can become part of what we offer others.

Pause & consider

Where would returning to study or seeking counsel serve the people who depend on you?
